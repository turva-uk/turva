# Clinical Safety

Turva is a tool for managing clinical safety evidence. That makes its own safety profile unusual: it never touches a patient, but a defect in it can cause an unsafe system to be deployed in the belief that it is safe. This file is the entry point for Turva's own clinical safety considerations.

This is a governance record maintained alongside the code. It is not a completed safety case, and it is not regulatory advice.

## What Turva does

- Stores, versions and structures clinical safety evidence: hazards, risk assessments, mitigations, and safety cases
- Enforces that risk level is derived from severity and likelihood rather than chosen
- Keeps an attributable, timestamped audit trail in Git
- Generates a safety case document (PDF) from that evidence

**AI status:** Assistance is planned, not implemented. [The AI strategy](specifications/llm-strategy.md) and [ADR 0007](specifications/adr/0007-user-and-organisation-supplied-inference.md) describe the intended user-/organisation-supplied access and the review gates that must precede enabling it.

## What Turva does not do

- It does not make clinical decisions and is not clinical decision support
- It does not monitor patients or process patient data
- It does not replace a Clinical Safety Officer, clinical competence, or professional judgement
- It does not certify that a system is safe. It helps a CSO construct and evidence that argument; the accountability remains the CSO's
- It does not guarantee regulatory compliance. It supports DCB0129 and DCB0160 processes

## Intended users and environment

Clinical Safety Officers and the development and deployment teams they work with, in NHS and NHS-adjacent organisations. Used in an office or remote-working setting on a general-purpose computer. Not used at the point of care, and not in a time-critical context.

TH-002 and TH-008 are the two that most directly undermine the product's purpose. TH-008 is the reason the risk matrix needs complete test coverage, as [specifications/architecture-principles.md](specifications/architecture-principles.md) already requires.

TH-010 and TH-011 are anticipated rather than present: they belong to capabilities 3 and 4 of [the funded scope](specifications/phase-one-scope.md), neither of which is built. They are recorded now because both are design constraints rather than defects to be found later. TH-010 says extraction must be presented as a proposal the CSO accepts against the visible source, never as a reading written straight into the hazard log, and that what could not be extracted must be reported rather than passed over. TH-011 says guided content must remain distinguishable from the CSO's own reasoning in the finished document - if the two are indistinguishable, nobody can later tell which parts anyone actually thought about. Both are a short step from TH-001 and TH-002, which are about the same underlying failure: a document that looks more complete, or more considered, than it is.

TH-009 was introduced deliberately, with mitigation, when profile and CSO designation were built. The alternative - not recording a registration at all - would have left the safety case saying _To be completed_ where a regulator expects a name and a registration number, which is worse. The controls are that the declaration is explicit rather than a casual checkbox, that it cannot be made without a recorded registration, that the interface marks the registration **Not verified by Turva**, and that the generated clinical risk management plan states the details were supplied by the named person and not checked against the public register. The residual risk is that a reader ignores all of that. Closing it properly needs either a register integration or an organisational counter-signature, and neither is in phase one.

The 15 November 2026 issue review retains that unresolved assurance policy as [R165](specifications/roadmap.md#clinical-governance-and-assurance) and [GitHub #3](https://github.com/turva-uk/turva/issues/3), separate from delivered email verification, profile recording and named-file authority. It does not claim professional verification has shipped, make a badge an authority grant or add automated checks to the funded port. The new matrix and AI connection-policy discussions ([#65](https://github.com/turva-uk/turva/issues/65), [#66](https://github.com/turva-uk/turva/issues/66)) are decision gates, not clinical acceptance; all relevant hazards remain open.

### Planned risk presentation and source review

On 15 November 2026, the roadmap source check compared `safety_file/risk.py` with Table 9, page 35 of the [DCB0160 implementation guidance v4.2, 2 May 2018](https://digital.nhs.uk/binaries/content/assets/website-assets/data-and-information/information-standards/standards-and-collections/dcb0160/0160252018impguid.pdf#page=35), source SHA-256 `4e2cff49e717e9344d7922fce4b47faaa47f80691663f84bab6733f6ac28462b`. Five cells differ: L2/S3, L3/S2, L3/S4, L3/S5 and L4/S3. For example, L3/S4 returns 4 in Turva and 3 in the published example. Section 4.4, page 33, explicitly makes these classifications illustrative and requires the organisation to document its chosen criteria; this comparison does not itself establish which matrix is clinically appropriate. [R17](specifications/roadmap.md#risk-presentation-and-matrix-assurance) requires a recorded CSO decision and independent source-derived vectors before any scoring change or claim that a display reproduces that example. R15's exact NHSE RGB palette and R16's optional initial/residual matrix markers remain planned presentation work, not shipped controls. No runtime scoring, rendering or historical evidence changed in this documentation update; TH-008 remains open.

### Planned optional AI wording assistance

The 15 November 2026 clarification makes AI optional and user-triggered: ordinary work needs no AI connection, and loading, typing or saving must not invoke inference. [R163](specifications/roadmap.md#ai-access) and [the wording-review contract](specifications/llm-strategy.md#optional-inline-wording-assistance) require field-level proposals with retained originals, red deletions/green additions and equivalent non-colour cues, plus accept/reject/edit/undo before a separate explicit Save. These remain design requirements, not shipped controls or clinical acceptance. No AI UI, inference or evidence-writing behaviour changed; the hazards remain open.

The managed browser does not expose native browser-menu zoom; independent native-zoom and screen-reader review remain part of R21, not a completed application-wide accessibility audit or clinical acceptance.

### Mitigation authoring and reciprocal-link evidence

Mitigation authoring initially used verified-owner access and now extends to named CSOs through the same mandatory opened-version/lock boundary as hazard entry (TH-004/TH-005). The storage writers, not the HTTP handlers, maintain hazard/mitigation reverse references and regenerate the log in one commit, including unlinking and many-to-many relationships. Existing inconsistent links stop saves for inspection instead of being silently repaired. Responsibility emails do not grant access; they are separate from organisational team grants and future mitigation-scoped delegation (TH-006). Status changes never alter a hazard's assessment automatically (TH-008), and implemented/verified mitigation saves need an evidence statement. That statement is a person's recorded judgement, not Turva testing a control or approving deployment (TH-001).

The new link tests exposed a shared frontmatter defect: removing the newline before the closing YAML delimiter changed the last block scalar's trailing newline. Parsing now preserves that delimiter-adjacent newline, with round-trip tests for evidence paragraphs (TH-003). Readers also refuse unrecognised hazard/mitigation/assessment fields, including Quill's extended version-1 provenance, rather than losing them on an unrelated save. This is a refusal, not an implemented import or schema migration (TH-010). Failed commits remain dirty for inspection; successful no-op forms create no version. Live fictional-file checks cover missing-evidence refusal, keyboard selection/removal, reciprocal links, unchanged risk, rename-following history, unchanged saves and retained two-tab conflicts. Phone/tablet/desktop inspection exposed and corrected truncated/over-compressed selected labels; the shared tables retain legible short identifiers/statuses alongside long narratives. The safety hazards remain open.

### Organisational collaboration and revocation evidence

Organisation administration, CSO appointment and named file authority are separate controls (TH-005/TH-006/TH-009). Operators provision organisations; invitations require the same verified email and grant membership only. Administrators can manage a file-title/team inventory but cannot read or edit private evidence without a current CSO appointment and explicit team grant. The Git version-2 record names one accountable CSO and its collaborators with a reason and attributable commit (TH-004); legacy files remain unchanged until explicit owner adoption. Appointment and declaration identities are checked on every evidence operation, including cached downloads. Reappointment/rejoining does not restore old grants. Responsibility emails remain recorded text, not invitations.

The service boundary now serialises organisation revocation against Git writes with PostgreSQL row locks, and pins the acting user row through the final permission check and commit so profile withdrawal cannot overtake an authorised save. Tests against PostgreSQL and real temporary Git repositories cover revocation, stale drafts/appointment choices, cross-organisation isolation, attribution, incomplete indexing, recovery without invented permissions, and deterministic row-lock contention. PDF permission is rechecked after rendering; the historical team is explicitly included and escaped, without claiming current competence or silently rewriting older narrative personnel details (TH-001/TH-007/TH-009). Live fictional tests confirmed colleague attribution, denial after role revocation, retained clinical drafts, no access after reappointment alone, and explicit grant renewal. This cannot retract already delivered evidence or prevent an operator bypassing Turva. Operational database state needs its own backup. See [ADR 0006](specifications/adr/0006-named-file-teams-and-live-appointments.md). Mitigation-scoped delegation remains unimplemented; the hazards remain open.

### Planned Kubernetes and Helm deployment

The MBPCC Kubernetes path is planned in [ADR 0008](specifications/adr/0008-kubernetes-and-helm-deployment.md). A PersistentVolume does not constitute a backup; consistent off-cluster copies and an isolated restore drill are required (TH-003). Interrupted saves may leave a dirty tree, which must be preserved for inspection. Every repository Job must obey the same lock rules as the application (TH-004). Git and PostgreSQL backups need compatible recovery points and live-access checks before restored evidence is served (TH-005).

One replica and `Recreate` are design choices, not proof that old and replacement writers cannot overlap. Follow-ups [#141](https://github.com/turva-uk/turva/issues/141) and [#142](https://github.com/turva-uk/turva/issues/142) must demonstrate the locking, recovery and backup controls on the chosen platform before production. This ADR changes no runtime control and closes no hazard.

### Dependency maintenance evidence

On 2026-11-10, Django was updated to 6.1.3, which fixes six security issues, including private-data cache isolation and password-hash disclosure in certain custom-user admin configurations. These upstream fixes support the controls for TH-005; they do not close it. The complete application suite, including authentication, CSRF and safety-file permissions, passed against PostgreSQL with Django 6.1.3, Python 3.14.9 and psycopg 3.3.8. See the [upstream release notes](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/6.1/releases/6.1.3/) for the affected configurations and limits of each fix. No risk calculation, audit attribution or permission policy was changed.

## Medical-device applicability

Required at inception as a governance record. **This record is incomplete and must be completed and dated by a named person before release.** A draft position is offered to start from; it is not a conclusion.

- **Intended purpose**: Management of clinical risk documentation and evidence by Clinical Safety Officers and development teams. No diagnosis, treatment, prevention, monitoring or prognosis of disease. No patient-specific output. No clinical claims about individual patients.
- **Decision-maker and role**: _To be completed._
- **Decision date**: _To be completed._
- **Next review date**: _To be completed._
- **Evidence considered**: _To be completed, with access dates._ Review MHRA guidance on [borderline products](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/borderline-products-how-to-tell-if-your-product-is-a-medical-device) and [regulating medical devices in the UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/regulating-medical-devices-in-the-uk), and NHS England guidance on [DCB0129 and DCB0160 applicability](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/clinical-safety/applicability-of-dcb-0129-and-dcb-0160/step-by-step-guidance).
- **Draft conclusion, for confirmation**: Turva appears not to meet the definition of a medical device, because it has no intended medical purpose in relation to an individual patient and produces no patient-specific output. It is a quality and documentation management tool supporting a clinical safety process.
- **Actions and owner**: _To be completed._ At minimum: name a clinical safety owner, confirm the conclusion, and set a review date.
- **Reassessment triggers**: Any change to intended purpose or clinical claims; introduction of patient-specific or patient-identifiable data; introduction of anything that could function as clinical decision support, including AI output that advises on individual patient care; integration with a medical device; change of target users or market.

Note that a conclusion of "not a medical device" would not remove the need to manage the hazards above. The two questions are separate.
